A stone in the hills of Japan has one rule carved into it: do not build your home below this point. You see, in 1896, a giant wave wiped out the little village of Aneyoshi. Only two people survived. Then, in 1933, it happened again. Four survivors. So the villagers carve a warning and set it high on the hillside. Over the next eighty years, the coast changes. Towns grow right down to the water, because that's where the boats are, and the jobs, and the view. All over Japan, old stones like this are forgotten. Just old rocks in the grass. But Aneyoshi's eleven families keep their houses up the hill, and walk down to their boats. Then, in 2011, the ground starts shaking. The strongest earthquake in Japan's recorded history sends a wave that swallows whole towns along the coast. It roars up the valley toward Aneyoshi... and stops about three hundred feet below the stone. Every single house is still standing.
