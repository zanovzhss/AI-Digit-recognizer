He won the lottery fourteen times, and he never once got lucky. You see, it's the 1960s, and Stefan Mandel is broke in communist Romania, when he notices something weird. If a jackpot gets big enough, buying every possible combination costs less than the prize. Everyone says it's crazy. You'd need millions of tickets. So his friends chip in, they fill out the tickets by hand... and they win. He moves to Australia and does it again. And again. Twelve times. Then, in 1992, he finds the perfect target. The Virginia jackpot hits twenty-seven million dollars, and there are only seven million combinations. Tickets cost one dollar. He prints them in a warehouse in Melbourne, ships a tonne of paper to America, and sends a small army of couriers into gas stations and grocery stores. They have three days. They run out of time before buying them all. Doesn't matter. The winning ticket is already in the pile. So every lottery in America changed the rules, so nobody could ever do it again. And Stefan? He retired to a tropical island.
